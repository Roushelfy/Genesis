# Full rigid Panda/egg stress recovery: measured results

Final held-out measurements are pending. This report draft records the frozen
method and completed calibration evidence; it does not assert full-goal
completion. The [acceptance audit](VALIDATION_RESULTS.md) links every completed
mathematical, physical and actual-scene check. Commands are in
[RUNNING.md](RUNNING.md).

## Fixed physical and numerical scope

The egg remains an ordinary rigid Genesis entity, driven by a Franka Panda.
Auxiliary quasi-static linear elasticity uses the complete fixed reference
shell, without symmetry, fixed contact positions or a restricted contact
basis. Every actual normal/tangential and table/support contact is mapped;
angular centrifugal load, gravity and consistent-mass inertia relief are
included. The global unaveraged VM maximum is reduced across every corner
of every affine P2 tetrahedron. Only a scalar per environment enters policy.

The final mechanical profile is full level 6, two wall layers, 0.5 mm wall
thickness, 2,457,630 DOFs and 491,520 tetrahedra. E=10 GPa, nu=0.3 and
density=2000 kg/m3 are declared model inputs. The rigid collision shell keeps
81,920 exterior triangles. It is convexified without decimation. The positive
surface quadrature order is 10; exact geometry indexing uses 3 mm cells.
All admitted samples remain in the footprint, with atomic scatter.

The pad law uses nonnegative compact Gaussian pressure and a constant
traction/normal ratio matching the actual force. Moving its exterior anchor
along the force line preserves the source point wrench. Radius varies with
environment and phase; 6 mm nominal, initial factors 0.8--1.2 and phase
modulation +/-15%, giving a minimum admitted demo radius of 4.08 mm. No force
clipping, friction increase or patch enlargement is used for numerical speed.

Rigid state and recovery are FP64. All matching scene scopes use dt=10 ms,
one substep, convex elliptic friction, 100 constraint iterations and 1e-12
tolerance, with contact pruning, hibernation and spin/rolling friction disabled.
The engine's effective contact time constant is 20 ms. The observer rejects
enabled spin/rolling friction because this GPU pad path takes point forces;
an admitted general pure-moment traction is independently tested on CPU.

The policy is a fixed untrained FP64 26->64 tanh->64 tanh->7 tanh MLP, seed 81,
with 1e-4 rad residual actions. Its stress observation stays on GPU and may
change those actions. This measures inference/rollouts, not PPO training.
Failed grasps remain in throughput counts. Numerical rejection produces a
NaN observation for the affected environment and fails benchmark acceptance.

## Hardware, factor sharing and timing

Measured hardware is **NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition**,
compute capability 12.0, 101,975,851,008 bytes VRAM (95 GiB), driver 595.71.05.
This is not RTX 6000 Ada. CUDA driver API/runtime versions are 13020/12090;
cuSPARSE reports integer version 12510. The CPU is Intel Xeon Platinum 8562Y+,
with eight allocated CPUs and OMP/OpenBLAS/MKL thread settings of one.

The [environment record](../evidence/20261009-performance/raw/20261009-quality/environment.json)
has Python 3.10.12 in `genesis:1_26`, NumPy 2.2.6, SciPy 1.15.3, Numba 0.68.0,
threadpoolctl 3.7.0, PyMetis 2025.2.2, CVXOPT 1.3.2, CuPy 14.2.0,
Torch 2.8.0, Quadrants 1.3.3, `nvidia-cudss-cu12` 0.8.0.10 and
`nvidia-cublas-cu12` 12.9.2.10. Runtime libraries are container provided;
absence of a separate CUDA runtime Python wheel does not mean CUDA is absent.

The native direct backend uses **one shared FP64 factor**, with
2,322,190,270 nonzeros and multiple analyzed RHS plans. Analysis/factorization
take approximately 14.5/9.6 s in calibration. Offline assembly, CPU oracle,
factorization, JIT, graph capture and visualization are outside steady-state
timing and reported separately. Factors are not duplicated per environment.
The exported SuperLU/cuSPARSE path remains available at supported sizes.
Fine native CPU CVXOPT solves remain the independent FP64 oracle; exported
fine CPU factors and the large SuperLU attempt encounter recorded memory
failures, so those exports are not claimed as successful fine backends.

Every primary repeat warms and then executes complete independently phased
600-step episodes plus reset, with at least ten seconds synchronized wall
time and three repeats. The largest initial delay approaches 600 steps, so
stress-bearing fine repeats naturally last many minutes. Aggregated throughput is total
environment transitions divided by total wall time; batch steps/s is that
quantity divided by B. Warmup/validation is excluded, but failed trajectories,
all physical substep recoveries, corrections, packing, resets and policy work
are included in their stated scopes. Device memory is total CUDA device-used
memory sampled at 10 Hz, including native/context allocations; it is a sampled
peak rather than an exact single-allocation maximum.

## Same-mesh numerical and physical accuracy

The held-out level-6 CPU mapping/direct comparison completes sixty frames,
stride 20, with 32 environment columns and saved identical complete RHS.
Maximum/p95/mean relative peak errors for reference >1 Pa are
**8.670e-12 / 2.095e-12 / 7.480e-13** (1,884 cases). Maximum absolute peak
error for reference <=1 Pa is **2.161e-9 Pa** (36 cases). Maximum full absolute
residual is 2.865e-12 N, relative residual above the absolute floor is
1.557e-10, and complete mapping/body RHS entry difference is 3.990e-17 N.
The [raw oracle](../evidence/20261009-performance/raw/20261009-heldout-oracle/strict-cpu.json)
and [summary](../evidence/20261009-performance/raw/20261009-heldout-oracle/summary.json)
pass both declared budgets. The sixty complete NPZ files remain on data,
35.543 GB compressed and 37.749 GB logical RHS, with a checked-in
[hash manifest](../evidence/20261009-performance/raw/20261009-heldout-oracle/rhs-manifest.json).
Full selected graph replay warmup will separately compare all 1,200 GPU frames
against eager FP64 direct recovery, retaining full gauge-row residuals.
CPU sampling is not described as 1,200 CPU frame solves. These are empirical
comparisons, not a rigorous universal peak-error certificate for unseen loads.

| Reported profile | Residual acceptance | Same-mesh peak budget, reference >1 Pa |
|---|---|---|
| Strict | abs <= max(1e-11 N, 1e-6*norm(RHS)) | max relative <=1e-4 (0.01%) |
| Throughput | abs <= max(1e-11 N, 1e-3*norm(RHS)) | max relative <=1e-2 (1%) |

Both profiles additionally check a 1e-3 Pa absolute budget for reference peak
<=1 Pa. The same strict FP64 runtime is eligible for both budgets. No faster
precision/fidelity variant is implied by the throughput label. Final reported
max/p95/mean relative errors exclude reference <=1 Pa, with absolute Pa errors
and counts published for that region. Relative residual maxima above the
absolute floor and absolute residuals are reported separately from near-zero
relative values. Runtime correction/fallback remains acceptance based. In direct mode,
`correction_environment_events` records every complete direct solve; numerical
acceptance and observation gating are reported separately.

The separate full-shell physical convergence suite uses levels 4/5/6 and
quadratures 6/10/16, unchanged footprints, twelve asymmetric loads and six
actual nominal trajectory snapshots. All eighteen final comparisons pass 2%:
maximum level-5-to-6 peak change **1.3645%**, quadrature-10-to-16 change
**8.412e-9 relative**. The independent one/two/four wall-layer sweep at level
4 gives maximum two-to-four-layer change **1.0625%**. These are representative
load checks, not a combined or universal physical accuracy certificate.
Raw point-anchor vs force-line anchoring remains a distinct load-model choice.

F/C assembled-RHS rates are 61.60/59.95 at B=8, 69.16/74.90 at B=32 and
93.84/107.67 at B=128. Layout benefit depends on batch size. Mapping-only
3 mm atomic/warp spans are 35.38/41.12 ms at B=8; wider 6 mm cells are slower.

measured body/inertia span from about 631 ms to 2.74 ms per B=8 batch;
pressure and sparse full recovery remain dominant. Final selected stages are
still pending and will be reported on fine held-out contacts.

The exact centrifugal cache is six geometry-derived nodal acceleration
fields, followed by the complete consistent mass action. It does not restrict
contacts or elastic displacement. TF32/Tensor Core performance is not claimed;
raw/scaled FP32 candidates are measured with their full refinement/fallback
cost and error. Temporal graph capture remains unsupported in the tested
CuPy cuBLAS path and is recorded as such; selected direct capture uses public
native calls and retained graph-compatible allocation pools.

## Actual trajectory quality and model limits

The nominal fine seed actually lifts and holds **1/1**, with a real
[video](../evidence/20261009-performance/visualization/nominal.mp4). The fine
32-seed held-out controller succeeds **0/32** on its keep-hold criterion.
The corresponding peak/force records include failures, birth/death, support,
slip, release and reset. Final timing warmups will additionally publish their
actual contact count histogram, radius/friction range and tangential forces.

Fine varied loads have maximum tangential force 1.52299 N and pressure moment
mismatch 9.13e-19 N m. Rigid Newton--Euler force/torque discrepancies are
6.34e-4 N / 1.47e-5 N m, while public constraint gradients agree to
7.01e-16 N / 4.33e-17 N m. Finite rigid convergence/integration discrepancies
are preserved in diagnostics. A real nonunit-normal constraint-frame defect
is fixed before rigid constraints are solved and has CPU/GPU regression tests.

This is fixed-material small-strain quasi-static recovery. It does not model
elastic waves, deformation feedback, fracture, buckling or biological failure
probability. The finite pressure footprint is an explicit additional model;
a rigid point resultant alone does not identify its uniquely correct pressure.
Changing material/topology requires compatible operator grouping or rebuild.
